### A.2 vs. US 10,839,401 B2 / US 2018/0211261 A1 (Honeywell — qualifying data from an unqualified system)

| Our contemplated limitation | Present in US10839401B2? | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Receiving data generated by an **unqualified** (e.g. ML/AI) system | **YES** | Explicit |
| **Verifying integrity** of that data before use | **YES** | Explicit |
| Passing **qualified** data to a **qualified** system in the vehicle | **YES** | Explicit |
| Integrity verification implemented as a **behavioural envelope test** (OOD score + calibrated uncertainty bound + robustness perturbation) against a defined operational design domain | **NO** | Reference is generic as to *how* integrity is verified |
| Emitting a **machine-readable evidence record** binding {input-window hash, model hash, gate decision, explanation artifact, standard-objective identifier} into an append-only store | **NO** | Reference produces qualified data, not certification artifacts |
| The qualified path **continues to operate deterministically** and the AI result is only **advisory** | **NO** | Reference contemplates qualified data being *provided to* the qualified system |
| **Deterministic replay** of the gate decision for a third-party auditor | **NO** | Not described |

**Conclusion:** do not claim “verify integrity then qualify then pass on”. Claim *the gate*, *the artifact*, and *the replay*.

## Part B — Draft independent claims

### Independent Claim 1 (Family 1 — cross-layer attribution; system)

A datalink diagnostic system comprising one or more ground-based servers including at least one processor and memory storing instructions that, when executed, cause the system to:

1. receive, over a ground network interface, a stream of **aircraft datalink transaction records**, each record identifying a transaction between a ground station and an aircraft communication management unit (CMU) and including per-leg transmission and reception timestamps;
2. receive, over the same or a different interface, **aircraft host-platform telemetry** for the aircraft associated with the transaction, the telemetry comprising at least one of: partition scheduling statistics of an avionics operating system, processor or memory utilisation of a communications application, data-link stack error counters, or device-driver fault records;
3. compute a **transaction delay budget** for the transaction by allocating measured delay to each leg of the transaction;
4. **correlate** the host-platform telemetry with the transaction delay budget over a time window aligned to the transaction by a common time reference, to determine an **attribution** that identifies whether the dominant contributor to the delay is (a) a ground-network leg, (b) an air-ground link leg, or (c) an **aircraft host-platform condition**;
5. generate a **machine-readable attribution record** comprising the attribution, the correlated telemetry values, a confidence measure, and a content hash over the inputs used;
6. **deterministically re-execute** the attribution from the stored attribution record and the content hash to reproduce an identical attribution, and mark the attribution record as verified for audit when the re-execution matches; and
7. cause at least one of: an alert, a datalink reconfiguration command, or a maintenance action, **based on the attribution**.

### Independent Claim 2 (Family 2 — certifiable AI placement; computer-implemented method)

A computer-implemented method of operating an AI-assisted datalink diagnostic function, comprising:

1. operating a **deterministic diagnostic path** that computes a datalink fault classification from the transaction records of claim 1 using rules and thresholds, without the AI model;
2. operating an **advisory path** in which a machine-learning model produces a candidate diagnosis for the same transaction;
3. applying an **admission gate** to the candidate diagnosis, the gate comprising: (i) an out-of-distribution score of the model input against a defined operational design domain, (ii) a calibrated uncertainty measure of the model output compared to a threshold, and (iii) a robustness perturbation test;
4. **admitting** the candidate diagnosis to an operational output only if the gate passes; otherwise outputting the deterministic diagnosis and recording a gate rejection;
5. writing an **evidence record** into an append-only, tamper-evident store, the evidence record binding: an identifier of the model and version, a hash of the input window, the gate inputs and decision, an explanation artifact generated for the admitted diagnosis, and an identifier of a **certification objective** of a software or hardware assurance standard to which the evidence record is mapped; and
6. generating, on request, a **certification evidence bundle** comprising a plurality of said evidence records aggregated by objective identifier.

### Independent Claim 3 (Family 3 — certification-gated threat-model distribution; system)

A method of maintaining interference/threat models for aircraft datalink, comprising:

1. collecting, at a ground service, datalink radio-frequency or transaction observations from a plurality of aircraft;
2. training or updating a **threat signature model** from the collected observations, the model being configured to classify interference or spoofing phenomena affecting aeronautical datalink;
3. **validating** the updated model against a frozen regression suite and a **certified operational envelope**, and rejecting the update if it exceeds an envelope bound or fails a regression case;
4. distributing an **accepted** model update to aircraft as **signed advisory threat data only**, without modifying airborne software subject to a design-assurance standard;
5. at the aircraft, evaluating the signed advisory threat data in a **certified, deterministic decision function** that independently decides whether to act; and
6. recording, for each distribution, a provenance record enabling **roll-back to a prior accepted model** and audit of which aircraft received which model version.

### Representative dependent claims (Family 1)

- The system of claim 1, wherein the common time reference is established by exchanging timing messages with the CMU and computing an offset per flight leg.
- … wherein the host-platform telemetry comprises ARINC-653-style partition timing information, and the attribution attributes delay to partition overrun when a partition overrun interval overlaps the transaction in-flight time.
- … wherein the datalink reconfiguration command switches subsequent transactions from a terrestrial VHF subnetwork to a satellite subnetwork, or vice versa.
- … wherein the attribution record is chained to a previously generated attribution record by a cryptographic hash to form an append-only audit trail.
- … wherein the transaction is a CPDLC or ADS-C message exchange and the delay budget is decomposed into a technical component and a **pilot operational response component**, and the attribution distinguishes a human-origin delay from a host-platform-origin delay.
- … wherein the confidence measure is computed as a function of the number of correlated samples in the time window and the clock-offset uncertainty.

### Representative dependent claims (Family 2)

- The method of claim 2, wherein the out-of-distribution score is computed in a latent space of the model and the uncertainty measure is a calibrated predictive interval.
- … wherein the explanation artifact is a surrogate-model decision path whose fidelity to the model output exceeds a threshold, and the fidelity value is stored in the evidence record.
- … wherein the certification objective identifier maps to a DO-178C, DO-254 or DO-330 objective, and the bundle is formatted as an objective-by-objective compliance matrix.
- … further comprising executing a **tool-qualification regression** on the gate, and including results of the regression in the bundle.
- … wherein the append-only store is hash-chained and the bundle includes a verification recipe enabling a third party to re-derive each record.

## Part C — Specification outline (skeleton with enablement hooks)

1. **Field** — aircraft datalink diagnostics; certification of AI-enabled avionics functions.
2. **Background / problem** — ground monitoring exists but attributes delay to network legs; operators cannot distinguish a network defect from an aircraft platform defect; AI cannot be placed in a certified path; and no existing monitoring output is admissible as certification evidence.
3. **Summary of the invention** — Families 1–3 as above.
4. **Figures** — (a) end-to-end transaction with per-leg timestamps and host telemetry overlay; (b) attribution engine block diagram; (c) dual-path architecture with admission gate; (d) evidence-record schema; (e) hash-chained store and replay; (f) threat-model learning and distribution loop with gate and roll-back; (g) UI showing attribution with evidence link.
5. **Detailed description**
   - 5.1 Data sources and the record schema for transaction legs.
   - 5.2 Time alignment: clock discipline, offset estimation, uncertainty propagation.
   - 5.3 Delay-budget allocation algorithm (with pseudocode).
   - 5.4 Host-platform telemetry collection on the aircraft allowed under the safety case (read-only, non-interfering).
   - 5.5 Attribution algorithm, including the decision among network / link / platform causes.
   - 5.6 Confidence computation and failure modes.
   - 5.7 The dual-path architecture and the exact admission gate (tables of thresholds; OOD method; calibration).
   - 5.8 Evidence schema (JSON example), objective mapping table (DO-178C tables A-3..A-9 analogues; DO-330 TQL2/TQL3 activities; DO-254 where the gate is implemented in hardware).
   - 5.9 Deterministic replay procedure and verification.
   - 5.10 Threat-model learning, envelope validation, signing, roll-back.
6. **Worked example with numbers** — e.g. a 30-day fleet window: X% of CPDLC exchanges exceeding 180 s; of those, Y% attributed to host-platform partition overruns, Z% to ground legs; gate rejected W% of AI diagnoses; bundle contained N evidence records mapped to M objectives.
7. **Claims** — as Part B.

## Part D — Drafting cautions

1. **Never** claim “detecting issues in datalink data using AI”, “explaining AI decisions”, or “monitoring latency against a threshold” as independent claims. All three are lost.
2. Keep **remediation inside Claim 1** (alert / reconfiguration / maintenance action). It is the single most useful hedge against a §101 abstract-idea rejection and it mirrors how the closest reference survived.
3. Put the **evidence schema and objective mapping in the specification**, not only in the claims — that is your §112 enablement insurance for Family 2.
4. Claim the **gate** before claiming the **bundle**. A competitor can copy a gate; a bundle alone is easy to design around.
5. Family 3 should **never** recite modifying certified airborne software. Advisory-only is the patentable *and* the certifiable position.
6. Consider a **defensive-publication fallback** for any element you decide not to file (e.g. the dashboard UX), to prevent others from claiming it against you.
